Altaleb (2024) – Integrating Technology and Social Media to Foster Language Learner Autonomy: Students’ Perception

Citation
- Altaleb, S. B. (2024). Integrating Technology and Social Media to Foster Language Learner Autonomy: Students’ Perception. Comprehensive Journal of Science, 9(33), 91–106.
- Article page: https://cjos.histr.edu.ly/index.php/journal/article/view/37
- PDF: https://cjos.histr.edu.ly/index.php/journal/article/view/37/29/64

Design and scope
- Sample: 30 Sabratha University English majors; 25 questionnaires returned; 6 semi-structured interviews.
- Mixed methods: quantitative items (autonomy readiness, ICT/social media usage) + qualitative interviews.

Key findings (attitudes, engagement, motivation)
- Strong positive attitudes toward autonomous learning; readiness to learn independently (83% prefer independent learning).
- Social media and ICT used extensively for academic purposes (Telegram, Facebook, Google, online resources) to prepare presentations, search references, collaborate.
- Outside-class activities: learning new words (76%), watching English films (73%), speaking to foreigners via social media (70%), using internet for chatting/surfing in English (80%), social networking (83%).

Exact quotes (with pages from journal pagination where identifiable)
- “The findings confirmed that Libyan university students place a high value on the significance of social media as a means to improve their English language skills and demonstrated a pronounced trend towards autonomous learning to learn English outside the classroom.” (Abstract, p. 91)
- “I use ICT to prepare for presentations in the classroom and to look up references.” (Interviewee 5; Results section ~ pp. 99–102)
- “I use internet to search for information related to my study on Google… I use social media.” (Interviewee 4; Results ~ pp. 99–102)

Implications (Libyan HE)
- Educators as facilitators: structure autonomy pathways via ICT-mediated tasks; blend in-class scaffolding with out-of-class autonomous activities.
- Policy: institutional support for ICT integration; reliable connectivity; training for the pedagogical use of social media.
- SDT alignment: autonomy (choice, self-direction), competence (ICT skill-building, task mastery), relatedness (peer collaboration via platforms).

Use in review
- Libya-focused empirical evidence connecting technology use to autonomous learning attitudes, engagement, and motivation; complements barriers noted in other Libyan studies (resources/ICT) with positive student readiness signals.
