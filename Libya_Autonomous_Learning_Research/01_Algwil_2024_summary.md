Algwil (2024) – Libyan EFL Teachers’ Beliefs about Learner Autonomy (Misurata University)

Citation
- Algwil, K. A. (2024). Libyan EFL Teachers’ Beliefs about Learner Autonomy at a Libyan University. Misurata University Journal (SATIL). 
- URL: https://journals.misuratau.edu.ly/satil/upload/file/R-2644-Libyan%20EFL%20Teachers%E2%80%99%20Beliefs%20about%20Learner%20Autonomy%20at%20a%20Libyan%20University.pdf

Design and scope
- Mixed-methods: questionnaire + semi-structured interviews with 40 Libyan EFL teachers (undergraduate context).
- Focus: teachers’ perceptions, strategies (organizer/facilitator/counselor/manager roles), and barriers to promoting learner autonomy.

Key findings (attitudes, engagement, motivation)
- Majority positive attitudes toward learner autonomy; use of student-centered methods, pair/group work, constructive feedback, and social participation (e.g., Telegram groups).
- Engagement via collaborative preparation, peer discussion, formative feedback cycles; decision-making in exam timing increased confidence and reduced stress.
- Motivation nurtured through supportive climate, autonomy scaffolding, clear instructions, and activities aligned to level; students’ sense of belonging correlates with effort and persistence.

Exact quotes (pages not indicated in scrape; verbatim lines retained)
- “When I ask my students to prepare presentation, some of them prepare slides and others are reluctant. They keep saying we know the evaluation will be mid-term 40 and final exam 60.” (Omer interview)
- “I create a safe and welcoming environment, and this reduces power relationship between me and my students. As a result, rapport of trust is established… [students] work hard and engage… If I ask them to prepare a piece of writing, they work on it enthusiastically and bring it on time.” (Sara interview)
- “I establish shared group on the telegram to exchange their thoughts and ideas and to share their knowledge.” (Questionnaire finding)
- “Students have the right to choose the suitable time of the mid-term exam… [build confidence, reduce fear, ownership of learning].” (Majd interview)
- Resource constraints: “projector was out of order… poor internet signals.” (Wael interview)

Barriers identified
- Exam-orientation and grade fixation suppress autonomous behaviors (performance over mastery).
- Limited independent learning skills (legacy of grammar-translation, rote traditions).
- Faculty rules/regulations entrench teacher-centered practices; limited autonomy for teachers and students.
- Insufficient resources: ICT, connectivity, audiovisual equipment.

Implications for Libyan HE (policy and practice)
- Institutional support for learner autonomy: flexibility in curricula, choice in assessment formats, formative feedback cycles.
- Teacher development: autonomy-supportive pedagogy, ICT integration (social media-mediated communities), explicit strategy instruction for self-regulation.
- Infrastructure: reliable ICT/connectivity; classroom technology maintenance.
- Encourage out-of-class language use and communities of practice (Telegram, social media) to enhance autonomy and engagement.

Use in review
- Provides direct Libyan evidence on attitudes, engagement, motivation, and barriers; integrates with SDT (autonomy/competence/relatedness) and constructivist social participation models.
