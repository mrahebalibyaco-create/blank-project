Saleh (2016) – Exploring EFL University Teachers’ Conceptions of Learner Autonomy (Zawia University Bulletin)

Citation
- Saleh, S. E. (2016). Exploring EFL University Teachers’ Conceptions of Learner Autonomy. University Bulletin – ISSUE No.18-Vol.(2) May 2016.
- PDF: https://bulletin.zu.edu.ly/issue_n18_2/Contents/E_11.pdf
- Page markers visible in PDF: -208-, -211-, -213-.

Design and scope
- Semi-structured questionnaire with 48 Libyan EFL university teachers (Zawia & Tripoli).
- Likert-style items adapted from Borg & Al-Busaidi (2012), plus open-ended definitions.

Key findings (attitudes, principles, practices)
- Positive views toward autonomy’s pedagogical principles; recognition of teacher’s role remains central.
- Strong rejection of misconception: “Learner autonomy means learning without a teacher.” (Item 8) [p. -211-]
- Acceptance: “Learner autonomy cannot develop without the help of the teacher.” (Item 18) [p. -211-]
- Agreement: “Learner-centred classrooms provide ideal conditions for developing learner autonomy.” (Item 28) [p. -213-]
- Support for out-of-class internet tasks and individual work (Items 21, 31, 30).

Obstacles and contextual issues
- Lack of facilities and poor library/self-access conditions (Libyan HE context).
- Tensions in power/responsibility: teachers hesitant to cede assessment/materials choice (Items 22, 27).
- Teacher-centered traditions, exam accountability, imposed materials hinder strong autonomy version.

Exact items and references (verbatim)
- “Learner autonomy means learning without a teacher.” (Rejected) [p. -211-]
- “Learner autonomy cannot develop without the help of the teacher.” (Agreed) [p. -211-]
- “Learner-centred classrooms provide ideal conditions for developing learner autonomy.” (Agreed) [p. -213-]

Implications for Libyan HE practice
- Promote weak/gradual autonomy model: shared decisions; increased student participation; scaffolding SDL skills.
- Interventions: self-access centers, improved internet access, group work structures, formative assessment, negotiated choice.

Use in review
- Demonstrates Libyan teachers’ nuanced conceptions: autonomy requires teacher facilitation; learner-centered environments are favorable; structural barriers remain salient.
