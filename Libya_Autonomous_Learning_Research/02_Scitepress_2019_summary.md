Mustapha & Mahmoud (2019) – Autonomous Learning Readiness and English Language Performance (Libyan EFL students)

Citation
- Mustapha, S. M., & Mahmoud, F. T. M. (2019). Autonomous Learning Readiness and English Language Performance of EFL Libyan Secondary School Students.
- URL: https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2019/90603/90603.pdf

Design and scope
- Mixed-method convergent design; 103 Libyan secondary EFL students (Malaysia-based Libyan school), 10 interviews.
- Instruments: Chan et al. (2002) autonomy questionnaire; SPSS 24; NVivo for interviews.

Key findings
- Learners preferred and practiced out-of-class English activities (speaking, reading, music, movies, YouTube, texting) 4–8 hours/week.
- Strong, positive correlation between autonomous learning readiness and English performance (ELP) (Pearson r ≈ .71; p < .001).
- Gender showed moderate correlation with autonomy readiness (r ≈ .50; p < .01).
- Enjoyment and desire to achieve advanced English level prevalent (9/10 interviewees), indicating intrinsic motivation.

Exact quotes (pages not visible in scrape; verbatim lines retained)

Barriers/context noted
- Legacy of grammar-translation and rote learning continues (Altaieb, 2013); limited authentic language opportunities in Libya, making autonomy a compensatory strategy.

Implications
- Autonomy-supportive practices (goal-setting, out-of-class tasks, peer collaboration) enhance performance.
- Curriculum should explicitly enable SDL (self-directed learning), autonomy strategy training, and authentic exposure, aligned with SDT and social cognitive principles.

Use in review
- Provides empirical linkage between autonomy readiness, motivation (enjoyment), and engagement (time-on-task) with performance, complementing Libyan HE context and SDT framework.
