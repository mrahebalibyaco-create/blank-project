World Bank (2007) – ICT in Education in Libya (infoDev/World Bank)

Citation
- Hamdy, A. (2007). ICT in Education in Libya. infoDev/World Bank, Country Report (Survey of ICT in Education in Africa).
- URL: https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/06932a88-87e3-5422-9c46-718e1d31e0b9/download

Scope
- Snapshot of ICT policy, infrastructure, and capacity-building initiatives; benchmarks pre-2011 context.

Exact excerpts (verbatim)
- “The national policy for ICT in education was launched in 2005 and is mainly managed by the Ministry of Education and the Ministry of Vocational Training… aims at enabling access to the ICT through the provision of computers and the Internet.”
- “There is a lack of well-qualified and trained teachers… required for building a generation of technically qualified students.”
- “Project activities [LHERN]… establishment of Local Area Networks (LANs) within all 149 faculties… Wide Area Network… digital libraries/portals… development of ICT-enhanced learning solutions (e.g., e-learning)… teacher training… automation of university management systems.”

Implications for autonomy in HE
- ICT policy foundations support out-of-class autonomous learning (e-learning, digital libraries).
- Persistent constraints (teacher training, infrastructure) explain uneven integration—aligns with 2024 findings on insufficient resources/ICT in classes.
- Opportunity: leverage LHERN-like initiatives for modern autonomy tools (learning analytics dashboards, self-access repositories).

Use in review
- Historical policy context; triangulates resource and training gaps; informs recommendations for ICT-enabled autonomy pathways in Libya’s HE.
